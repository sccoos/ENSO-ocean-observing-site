## Subsurface Conditions: Moorings

**Conditions summary:** At the M1 mooring in Monterey Bay, temperatures have run above average through the water column since mid-to-late 2025, with anomalies at 20 m mostly 1–2°C above normal and only brief dips toward average. This persistent subsurface warmth matches the warm conditions at Central California shore stations and in glider surveys, showing the heat extends well below the surface. Anomalies so far are about half of those seen during the 1997–98 El Niño and the 2014–15 marine heatwave, so there is room for further warming as El Niño's effects reach the coast this winter.

**About the data:** The M1 mooring sits about 20 km offshore in Monterey Bay and has been operated by MBARI, with CeNCOOS support, since the late 1980s, making it one of the longest-running ocean moorings on the U.S. West Coast. Sensors at fixed depths from near the surface down through the upper water column record temperature continuously. The plot shows anomalies, meaning how much warmer or cooler each depth is than its long-term average for that time of year.
