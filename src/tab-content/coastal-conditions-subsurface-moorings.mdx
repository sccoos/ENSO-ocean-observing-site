## Subsurface Conditions: Moorings

<article class="rounded-xl border border-layer-line bg-layer p-5">
  <p class="text-muted-foreground"><strong>Conditions summary:</strong> Temperatures at M1 have been above average through the water column since mid-to-late 2025, first from the marine heatwave and now from El Niño. Since early October the warming has sharpened. Records fell at 60 m (19.22 °C, Nov 10), 100 m (17.89 °C, Nov 5) and the surface (19.61 °C, Nov 1). Anomalies at 60 m are now near 8 °C, beyond those of the 1997–98 El Niño. In the 1997–98 event, M1 warming peaked in mid-to-late November, so the next few weeks will show whether 2026 keeps climbing.</p>

<article class="rounded-xl border border-layer-line bg-layer p-5">
  <p class="text-muted-foreground"><strong>About the data:</strong> The M1 mooring sits about 20 km offshore in Monterey Bay and has been operated by MBARI, with CeNCOOS support, since the late 1980s, making it one of the longest-running ocean moorings on the U.S. West Coast. Sensors at fixed depths from near the surface down through the upper water column record temperature continuously. The plot shows anomalies, meaning how much warmer or cooler each depth is than its long-term average for that time of year.</p>
  <p class="mt-4 text-muted-foreground">The Del Mar Mooring is located off Del Mar, California, and is led by principal investigator Uwe Send of Scripps Institution of Oceanography. It was developed in 2006 with the Scripps Hydraulics Laboratory as part of SCCOOS. Its instruments measure temperature, salinity, oxygen, currents, and other ocean and atmospheric conditions.</p>
