<p class="text-muted-foreground"><strong>About the data:</strong></p>
  <p class="text-muted-foreground"><strong>Automated shore stations -</strong> Stations on piers, wharves and other coastal sites (such as Humboldt, Santa Cruz Wharf and Scripps Pier) measure nearshore seawater temperature every few minutes, as part of the CeNCOOS and SCCOOS networks. Each plot compares recent readings with the station's long-term record: the average, the minimum and maximum, and the 90th percentile, which is the usual threshold for a marine heatwave. Data are quality-controlled following national (QARTOD) standards, and full records are available on the <a href="https://data.caloos.org">CalOOS Data Portal</a>.</p>
  <p class="text-muted-foreground"><strong>Manual shore stations -</strong> The Scripps Institution of Oceanography Shore Stations Program runs one of the longest coastal ocean temperature records in the world. Staff and volunteers take readings by hand every day from piers and islands along the coast. At Scripps Pier, Birch Aquarium and Scripps staff have sampled surface temperature since 1916 and bottom temperature (about 5 m) since 1926. At Southeast Farallon Island, Point Blue Conservation Science biologists have taken daily readings since 1925. More than a century of records puts this year’s temperatures in historical context and lets us count daily records. Data and plots: <a href="https://shorestations.ucsd.edu">shorestations.ucsd.edu</a>.</p>
