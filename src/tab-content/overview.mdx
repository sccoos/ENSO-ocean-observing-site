      <p class="mb-3 text-sm italic text-muted-foreground">Updated: October 7th, 2026</p>
      <ul class="overview-card__list">
        <li>A persistent warm anomaly has been observed along much of the coast data back to September 2025 (see <a href="#coastal-conditions">Coastal Conditions</a>, <a href="#offshore-conditions">Offshore Conditions</a>).</li>
        <li>El Niño temperature signals evident at Southern California, both at shore stations and along the glider lines (see <a href="#offshore-conditions/gliders">Gliders</a>).</li>
        <li>In Central and Northern California, upwelling has brought short bursts of cooling. These dips interrupt the warm anomaly but haven't reversed it (see <a href="#upwelling/gliders">Upwelling</a>).</li>
        <li>Much of this warmth started as a marine heatwave before El Niño arrived. Earlier this year, glider temperature anomalies on Lines 66.7 and 80 were the largest in the 20-year glider record.</li>
