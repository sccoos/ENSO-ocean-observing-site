<div class="rounded-xl border border-layer-line bg-layer p-5">
  <p class="text-muted-foreground"><strong>Conditions summary:</strong> El Niño has now reached the glider lines off Central and Northern California, not only Line 90 off Southern California. The Trinidad Head Line survey (Oct 3–19) found warm anomalies along the whole surveyed section, out to about 220 km offshore. The strongest warming was 3–4 °C at 40–60 m depth, about 90–120 km offshore. Water over the continental slope, within about 50 km of shore, was 1–1.5 °C warmer than normal from the surface to 500 m. This deep warming hugs the coast, which fits El Niño heat arriving as waves moving north along the shore. Earlier this year, anomalies on Lines 66.7 (Monterey Bay) and 80 (Point Conception) were the largest in the 20-year glider record.</p>

<div class="rounded-xl border border-layer-line bg-layer p-5">
  <p class="text-muted-foreground"><strong>About the data:</strong> The California Underwater Glider Network uses autonomous Spray gliders, operated by Scripps Institution of Oceanography, to survey fixed lines running hundreds of kilometers offshore. The gliders dive repeatedly from the surface to depth, measuring temperature, salinity and other properties. Several lines have been sampled almost continuously since the mid-2000s (Rudnick et al. 2017).</p>
  <p class="mt-4 text-muted-foreground">The Trinidad Head Line off Humboldt County, Northern California, has been surveyed by Spray glider since 2014. THL Glider is operated by Oregon State University with support from CeNCOOS and NANOOS.</p>
