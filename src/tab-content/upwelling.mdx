  <p class="text-muted-foreground"><strong>Conditions summary:</strong> Coastal upwelling has mostly stopped for the season, and on the North Coast winds have reversed to drive downwelling. Off Northern California and the Oregon border (39–42°N), early October brought some strong late-season upwelling, which explains the brief cool-downs at shore stations. Since early November, storms and southerly winds from weather systems moving in from the North Pacific have reversed that, pushing surface water toward shore and down (downwelling). Downwelling off the Oregon border on Nov 9–11 was about two to three times stronger than usual for early November. Off Mendocino, a strong storm on Nov 3 drove the largest downwelling of the season. Upwelling off Central California is weak, near or slightly below normal for this time of year, and off Southern California it has been near zero since early November.</p>

  <p class="text-muted-foreground"><strong>About the data:</strong> The Coastal Upwelling Transport Index (CUTI) and Biologically Effective Upwelling Transport Index (BEUTI) were developed by NOAA's Southwest Fisheries Science Center (Jacox et al. 2018). They're calculated daily at each degree of latitude along the West Coast from a data-assimilating ocean model. CUTI improves on the older Bakun index.</p>
